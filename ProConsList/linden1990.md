# linden1990: Emptying filling boxes: the fluid mechanics of natural ventilation

**Source:** https://doi.org/10.1017/S0022112090001987
**Evidence boundary:** Experimental and theoretical fluid mechanics paper. Read in full from a scanned reprint (AIVC airbase 4687); OCR quality is poor, so no equation layouts are quoted.

## Sensors

None. Salt-water tank experiments with dense fluid standing in for heat.

### Pros

- Clean separation of displacement (low inlet, high outlet) and mixing ventilation.

### Cons

- No air, wind, screens or rain.

## Physical Box, Materials, and Geometry

Perspex box 25 cm high, 30 cm long, 20 cm wide in a 0.6 x 0.6 x 1.3 m tank, with 1.8 cm or 5.5 cm holes in top and bottom that could be plugged (Sec. 4).

### Pros

- Flow scales with an effective area A* times the square root of the height between openings; A* is set mainly by the smaller of the top and bottom openings (text after eq. 2.4).
- Horizontal vents were about 5 times less effective than vertical ones in mixing mode (k = 0.05 against 0.25; Secs. 6-7).

### Cons

- Ideal turbulent orifice model. Millimetre holes at low pressure may be laminar.
- Heat source is a plume; a sensor box is mainly heated through its walls by the sun.

## Selection Lessons for This Project

- Supports equal inlet and outlet group areas and vertical separation between them.
- Cannot give flow rates for the lab box; Sec. 6 itself notes that wind pressure usually exceeds stack pressure.
